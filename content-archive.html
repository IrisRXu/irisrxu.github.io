About Me

Hi! I am Iris Xu. I am a graudating computer science student at Columbia University in NYC. In my current role as a research intern at Columbia Computer Enabled Abilities Lab (CEAL), I work on building AR social navigation assistant on XR headset that makes social interaction more accessible for people with visual impairements. I think deeply about how AI systems move from models and infrastructure into lived user experience, and what it means to design technology that directly supports people in navigating the world around them.

Previously, I developed iOS mobile augmented reality applications for smart city projects that explored how digital information can be meaningfully embedded into physical space.

Apart from research and programming, I especially enjoy visual and media arts, particularly in film and photography. I also spend time knitting, bouldering, and exploring the city.

Projects

XR Research Intern — Audio Augmented Reality for Social Navigation

Computer Enabled Abilities Lab (CEAL), Columbia University

Social interaction in physical spaces can be difficult to navigate without visual cues. I work on extended reality systems that help make social environments more perceivable and interactive for people with visual impairments, building infrastructure that connects real-time perception with spatial audio feedback. Our prototype runs on Quest 3.

Unity, C#, Google Cloud Platform, WebSocket

iOS AR Research Intern — Augmented Reality for Smart City

Computer Enabled Abilities Lab (CEAL), Columbia University

Digital content often feels detached from the physical spaces it relates to. I developed a mobile augmented reality application that allows users to anchor and view virtual content directly within their surroundings. The project explored how spatial computing can support shared expression and persistent digital layers in everyday environments.

iOS development, ARKit, Swift, Figma

Research Intern — Computational Fabrics for Sleep Monitoring

MobileX Lab, Columbia University

Traditional sleep monitoring systems can be intrusive and uncomfortable. I worked on designing and fabricating a computational fabric sensor array that captures physiological signals via skin-contact. The system achieved strong posture classification performance and clinically comparable vitals prediction, and iterative redesign improved comfort and signal stability.

Digital fabrication, PCB design

VR Auditory Interaction Prototype

In virtual environments, sound is powerful but often hard to control in intuitive ways. I designed and built a VR prototype that lets users adjust spatial audio sources naturally through hand-based interaction. The project explored how embodied gestures can make audio manipulation feel physical and immediate.

Unity, C#, Figma

Interactive Poetry Learning Platform

Poetry can feel distant or intimidating without the right entry point. I created an interactive web platform that invites users to explore poems through guided exercises and modular, self-directed discovery. The result was a lightweight learning environment designed to make literature feel more approachable and participatory.

HTML/CSS, JavaScript, Flask, Figma

Emotions through Art

Art interpretation is deeply personal, but digital collections often feel static. I built an interactive web experience that uses AI to help users explore and reflect on artwork through semantic interpretation. The project connected language models with museum data to create a more conversational, exploratory experience.

Python, JavaScript, Flask, Figma, OpenAI API

Liquid Classification & Area Prediction

Understanding material properties through sensing data can unlock new applications in embedded systems. I trained a CNN that classifies liquids based on electrical impedance signals and predicts their physical characteristics. The project demonstrated how signal processing and learning models can translate raw sensor data into meaningful distinctions.

Python, Seaborn, Scikit-learn, TensorFlow
